Refund & Cancellation Policy

Last updated: November 2026

The short version: if you're unhappy, email hello@ozzily.com within 30 days of a charge and we'll refund it in full. Cancelling stops future charges. Replying STOP stops the texts, but it does not cancel billing — those are two different things.

1. The 30-day refund window

If you've been charged and Ozzily isn't working for you, you can ask for a refund within 30 days of that charge. We'll refund it in full. You don't have to explain yourself, and there's no retention flow to sit through.

  • Monthly plan: the window runs 30 days from each monthly charge.
  • Annual plan: the window runs 30 days from the annual charge, including a renewal charge.

The 30 days are counted from the date of the charge, not from the date you signed up.

2. How to request a refund

Email hello@ozzily.com from any address and tell us the phone number you use with Ozzily. That number is how we find your account, because it's the only identifier we collect at signup.

We aim to reply within two business days.

3. What happens after we approve a refund

Refunds are issued by Paddle, which is the merchant of record for your purchase and the company that took the payment. The money goes back to the original payment method — we cannot send it anywhere else.

How long it takes to appear is up to your bank or card issuer, and is usually a few business days after Paddle processes it. Paddle will email you when the refund is on its way.

A refund also ends your subscription, so you won't be charged again.

4. Cancelling, refunding, and STOP are three different things

These get confused often enough that it's worth being exact:

What you doWhat it does
Email us to cancelStops all future charges. You keep access as described below.
Email us for a refundReturns a charge you've already paid, within the 30-day window, and ends the subscription.
Reply STOP to a textStops the text messages. It does not cancel your subscription and does not stop billing.

STOP is a messaging control required by mobile carriers, and it works instantly — but it only governs the texts. If you reply STOP and do nothing else, your subscription stays active and you'll keep being charged. To stop the money, email hello@ozzily.com.

5. How to cancel

Email hello@ozzily.com with the phone number you use with Ozzily and ask us to cancel. We'll confirm by email once it's done.

  • Monthly plan: cancelling stops future billing. The period you've already paid for runs to its end, and we don't prorate a partial month — but if you're inside the 30-day window, you can ask for a refund of that charge instead.
  • Annual plan: cancelling stops the renewal, and you keep access through the year you've already paid for. Again, we don't prorate — and again, the 30-day refund window applies to the charge itself.

So "no proration" and "30-day refunds" are not in conflict: proration is about splitting a period you've chosen to keep, and the refund window is about returning a charge outright.

6. The free trial

The 3-day trial takes no card and costs nothing, so there's nothing to refund. If you never pay, you're never charged. On day 4 we text you a payment link, and you're only charged if you use it.

Paddle handles order processing, payments, tax, customer service enquiries about your order, and returns and refunds. Paddle is the seller named on your receipt and on your card or bank statement, and it collects and remits any applicable sales tax or VAT. Prices shown include tax, so the amount you see is the amount you pay.

7. When we may decline a refund

We'd rather just refund you than argue, so this list is short and we apply it narrowly:

  • The charge is more than 30 days old. Cancelling still works at any time.
  • We've already refunded the same charge.
  • The account is being used to abuse the service — spamming it, trying to break it, or repeatedly subscribing and refunding.

None of this limits any refund or cancellation right you have under the consumer law where you live, and nothing here is intended to take those rights away. Where your local law gives you more than this policy does, your local law wins.

If we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top. A change never applies retroactively to a charge you've already paid — the policy that was published when you were charged is the one that governs it.
